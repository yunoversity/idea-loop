    Footnote: how the composite score and multipliers work
    1. Inputs (1–5). Each value on each dimension gets Market demand (D), Enterprise fit (E) and Difficulty to source (H). D and E: 5 = needs attention most. H: 1 = easy, 5 = very hard.
    2. Flip difficulty. Ease of sourcing S = 6 − H, so an easy gap scores higher.
    3. Cell inputs. A gap is a cell of two values (X, Y). Each input is the mean of the two values’ scores: D = (Dx + Dy) / 2, and likewise E and S.
    4. Criteria multipliers (weights). Base = (wD·D + wE·E + wS·S) / (wD + wE + wS), on a 1–5 scale. Weights default to 40 / 35 / 25 and you can change them above.
    5. Scale to 0–100. Priority = (Base − 1) / 4 × 100.
    6. Coverage multiplier. A thinly covered cell is a weaker gap than an empty one: 0 voices ×1.00, 1 voice ×0.60, 2 voices ×0.30, 3 or more ×0 (not a gap).
    7. Composite = Priority × Coverage multiplier. Gaps are sorted by composite, highest first. Ties go to the cell with fewer voices, then alphabetically.

    Cost is deliberately outside this analysis; “difficulty to source” is your judgment of casting and recording effort only. Catalog labels (accent, age, tone, use case) are Deepgram’s own and unverified. Values marked “not in catalog” are hypotheses you can score, not existing labels. “Illustrative scores” are placeholders to show how ranking works, not research findings.
