# Red Pen

**The editor that catches "sounds like a template" before the hiring manager does.**

---

## The problem

When job hunters finally work up the nerve to message someone, the message often sinks them: it opens like a form letter, hides a favor request behind flattery, and says nothing specific about the person receiving it. The reader deletes it in four seconds — and the hunter never learns why.

---

## Who has this problem

Mid-career tech professionals in an active search. Our two pilot users write real outreach today and get silence back. Each message is a small act of courage, and there's no feedback loop — no one ever tells you your opener sounded like everyone else's.

---

## How they cope today — and why it fails

1. **Guess and send.** No way to know what went wrong; silence teaches nothing.
2. **Ask a friend to read it.** Friends are polite. Polite feedback doesn't fix messages.
3. **Have AI rewrite it.** Now it *definitely* sounds like a template — the reader can tell, and the hunter's own voice is gone.

---

## The insight

The difference between a message that gets answered and one that gets deleted is a handful of detectable mistakes: template phrases, asking before giving, zero specifics about the reader. You don't need AI to write for people. You need it to catch these mistakes — and make the human's own words better.

---

## What Red Pen does

Paste your draft and one line about who it's for. You get margin notes, never a rewrite:

- **"This opener appears in ten thousand LinkedIn messages."**
- **"Paragraph two is secretly a favor request. Where's the gift?"**
- **"You never mention anything specific about *them*. She gave a podcast interview last month — listen to ten minutes first."**

You revise in your own voice. After twenty critiques, you don't need the tool anymore — you've internalized the red pen. We're fine with that.

---

## Why now

AI-written outreach is exploding, so readers have become expert template-detectors overnight. The bar for "sounds human" is rising every month. A tool that keeps you *under* the radar by keeping you genuinely human gets more valuable exactly as the flood gets worse.

---

## Why we're uniquely positioned

- **We are the user** — both pilots are writing real outreach in live job searches right now.
- **The critique rules are earned, not scraped.** They come from years of our own outreach that worked and failed: cap the flattery at one sentence, ask questions instead of favors, never open with the reader's problems. That playbook is in the product from day one.
- **We ship in 48 hours** — the whole product is one paste box and a sharp eye, and our build system can have it live for pilots this week.
- **We will never add the "just write it for me" button.** That's the discipline every competitor breaks. It's also the entire reason the product works.

---

## What we'll prove first

Take real messages our pilots already sent, run them through Red Pen, and have neutral readers judge original vs. revised without knowing which is which. If revised doesn't clearly win, we stop. If it wins, the harder question: do pilots *send more messages* when critique is available — or was confidence never the bottleneck?
