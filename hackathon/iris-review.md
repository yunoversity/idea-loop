# Iris review — hackathon decks (2026-08-22)

Read: all three v2 decks, the PRD, goals.md. Written per working-style.md:
headlines first, direct but constructive. This is a recommendation, not a
decision — the call is Anthony's.

## Dossier (A) — strongest concept, heaviest build, one hidden dependency

**Strengths**
- Attacks the bottleneck both pilots actually named: research time per message.
- Output IS the value-add ("feel heard" manufactured from real attention) —
  the tightest fit to the PRD's core insight and Anthony's hypothesis of record.
- Fails soft: even an unsent brief teaches the user something real.
- Gated by a free afternoon test (A3: is there enough public material?).

**Uncertainties**
- Heaviest engineering of the three; the "90 seconds" promise is the hard part
  of the 48h scope, not the UI.
- Hidden dependency: a great brief only matters if messages get SENT. If the
  true blocker is courage rather than research time, briefs pile up unread —
  and nothing in Demo A tests that.
- Quiet targets (thin public footprint) may produce thin briefs; the tool must
  say "not enough signal" honestly or trust dies.

## Red Pen (B) — cheapest test, real moat in the rules, lowest ceiling

**Strengths**
- Smallest build by far; the blind before/after judgment is fast and clean.
- Anthony's earned outreach rules are a genuine, hard-to-copy asset — this
  deck productizes them most directly.
- Zero genuineness risk (never generates a word) and it teaches a permanent
  skill.

**Uncertainties**
- Only improves messages that get written — and our pilots currently write
  few. It sharpens the sword of someone already swinging.
- A quality delta without a send-rate change proves craft, not business.
- Critique quality is the entire product; one generic note kills trust in
  one use.

## Warm Path (C) — tests the decisive unknown, magic depends on manual hooks

**Strengths**
- Aims at the PRD's most consequential open question: is the blocker
  activation energy (courage/friction) or research time? One pilot week
  answers it with behavior, not opinions.
- Zero content generation → zero genuineness risk; "two cards max" is a real
  product insight.
- Medium build; behavior data within days of shipping.

**Uncertainties**
- The why-now hook is the soul of the product, and at MVP it's MANUAL —
  users supply their own hooks, which may gut the magic.
- Habit products flatter themselves in week one; honest judgment needs two.
- Contact import is the first impression; a tedious import loses both pilots
  in an hour.

## The shape of the choice

The three decks test different layers of the same funnel:
**C tests whether outreach happens at all · A tests whether there's something
real to say · B tests how well it's said.** The PRD's riskiest unknown is the
first one — if courage is the blocker, the other two build on sand.

## Recommended path

1. **This weekend, before any build (both free):**
   - Run the A3 desk test: 10 real targets from Anthony + Kevin's lists; count
     how many yield genuine value-add material.
   - Run a manual Red Pen: the team applies the critique rules by hand to 5 of
     the pilots' real past messages; neutral readers judge original vs.
     revised blind. This tests B's core claim with zero code.
2. **First /build: Warm Path (C).** It resolves the decisive unknown with one
   week of real behavior, carries no genuineness risk, and fits the 48h SLA
   most comfortably. Its result makes every later decision smarter.
3. **Second build: Dossier (A), if A3 passed** — now informed by C's answer on
   whether briefs will actually get used. **Fold Red Pen's rules into A** as
   the brief's "message guidance" panel rather than a standalone product.

**Switch trigger (decide before, not after):** if the weekend evidence shows
research-fuel is rich (A3 ≥ 8/10) AND both pilots say plainly they'd use briefs
on their hardest day, flip the order — A first, C second. Anthony's conviction
plus strong fuel beats sequencing theory.

**What I'd not do:** build B standalone first. Cheapest is tempting, but its
ceiling is lowest and its core claim is testable this weekend for free.
