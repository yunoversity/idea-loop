# PRD: Cold-apply fatigue — genuine network outreach for job hunters

- **Painpoint:** painpoints/pp-2026-08-22-24-cold-apply-fatigue.md
- **Persona:** Mid-career tech professional, actively job hunting (often while
  in a disengaging current role)
- **Graduated:** 2026-08-22 (Anthony's stated reason: evidence-ready with
  first-hand pilots — himself and Kevin Purdy — fast-tracked ahead of meeting)
- **Author:** Athena · **Reviewed/edited:** Iris (see review log at bottom)
- **Pilots:** Anthony Yun, Kevin Purdy

## Root-cause driver tree

**Felt pain:** exhausted by cold applying; knows network outreach works better;
doesn't do enough of it; coffee chats rare; burnout risk compounding.

1. **Why do hunters keep cold applying though it doesn't work?**
   - It *feels like progress without results* [observed — Anthony's words]:
     countable actions, zero emotional risk per action.
   - Outreach is the opposite: high effort AND high emotional exposure per
     contact [assumed — emotional component not yet confirmed by pilots].
   - → **Root cause R4 (emotional asymmetry):** the low-risk false-progress
     channel structurally outcompetes the high-risk effective channel for a
     tired person's next hour.

2. **Why doesn't genuine outreach scale?**
   - A genuine message requires per-recipient research and craft
     [observed — workaround: hand-crafted messages work but don't scale].
   - Research is manual and slow; LinkedIn prohibits automated scraping, so
     the obvious automation is off the table [observed constraint].
   - Templated AI messages backfire — recipients read them as low-effort
     [observed, both-sides].
   - → **Root cause R1 (costly-signal economics):** outreach works *because*
     it proves invested attention. Any shortcut that removes the visible cost
     removes the signal. Automation that fakes effort is detected and
     counterfeits the currency.
   - → **Root cause R2 (research friction):** the legitimate cost — learning
     enough about a person to say something that matters — has no scaffolding
     today. It's the bottleneck between "knows outreach works" and "does it."

3. **Why are coffee chats rare even when outreach is sent?**
   - Standard outreach opens with an ask (time, referral, "pick your brain")
     and offers nothing [observed mechanics: what works is interest-not-ask
     plus immediate value-add].
   - → **Root cause R3 (reciprocity deficit):** the message asks before it
     gives. Value-add — delight, feeling heard, workday relief [observed,
     Anthony's definition] — flips the exchange; "feel heard" in particular is
     produced by demonstrating real attention to the person's work (R1 and R3
     are two faces of the same currency).

4. **Why burnout risk?** Search hours stack on top of a disengaging day job;
   the false-progress treadmill delivers no wins to recover on [observed].

## Workaround landscape (and what each proves)

| Workaround | What its existence proves |
|---|---|
| Cold applying anyway | Demand for *feeling* of progress; the lowest-friction channel wins even when known-ineffective (R4) |
| Hand-crafted outreach | Hunters know outreach works and will pay real effort at high stakes; failure to scale locates the bottleneck at research/craft time (R2) |
| AI-templated blasts | Demand for scaling outreach exists; its backfiring is direct evidence for costly-signal economics (R1) |
| Rare coffee chats despite effort | Current ask-first framing doesn't convert (R3) |

## The do-nothing path

The search stretches; burnout compounds against the disengaging day job; the
hunter settles for a suboptimal role or abandons the move. Because mid-career
professionals search multiple times per career, the pain *recurs and compounds*
— this is not a live-with-it-forever pain, it's a pay-it-every-cycle pain.
Additionally each failed cycle erodes the network confidence that outreach
depends on.

## Assumptions register (ranked by blast radius; each with its designed test)

| # | Assumption | If wrong… | Cheapest test | Signal & threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Research-informed, HUMAN-written outreach is received as genuine (unlike templated) | Whole approach dies — genuineness can't be tool-assisted at all | Pilots send research-briefed messages to real targets during their live search | Response quality/rate vs. their own baseline; any unprompted "thoughtful note" reaction = Strong |
| A3* | Enough ToS-clean public signal exists per target (blogs, talks, GitHub, podcasts, company posts) to fuel value-add research for typical mid-career target lists | Tool has no fuel; R2 unsolvable this way | **Desk test, pre-build:** sample 10 real targets from pilots' lists; count usable-material hit rate | ≥7/10 targets yield a genuine value-add angle = pass; ≤4 = stop and rethink |
| A2 | The felt bottleneck is research/craft time, not emotional risk | Tool gets used once, behavior doesn't change (R4 dominates) | Kevin kit interview + pilot usage: does cheap research actually increase messages sent? | Pilots send ≥2x their baseline outreach in week 1 = Strong |
| A4 | The three value-add modes (delight / feel heard / workday relief) are producible from research briefs | Messages informed but not valuable; conversion unchanged | Blind read: recipients-adjacent reviewers judge briefed drafts vs. unbriefed | Majority judged "genuine + valuable" = pass |
| A5 | Interest-not-ask openers convert to actual conversations | Value delivered but no coffee chats; R3 misdiagnosed | Lagging: chats booked per 10 briefed messages vs. baseline | Any improvement over near-zero baseline = directional pass |
| A6 | Pilots will use the tool inside a live search week | Workflow-fit failure regardless of concept | 1-week usage by both pilots, no reminders after day 2 | Unprompted use on ≥3 days = pass |

\* A3 listed second despite mid blast radius — see Iris's sequencing edit below.

## Directions worth exploring (deliberately thin — not a spec)

- **Research-briefing dossier:** given a target the user names, assemble a
  ToS-clean brief (their public work, recent output, plausible common ground)
  plus candidate value-add angles per the three modes. Human writes the message.
- **Draft-critic:** "this sentence reads templated / this is an ask, not an
  interest" — AI as the genuineness police, never the author.
- **Target sequencing:** order the user's own contact list by warmth ×
  freshness of common ground, so the next hour of courage is spent well.

Constraints carried from capture: **no LinkedIn scraping** (user-provided and
public non-restricted sources only); the human always writes and sends —
the tool must never send messages.
